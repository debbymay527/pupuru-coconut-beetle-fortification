Quality Evaluation of Pupuru Fortified with Coconut Beetle Larvae Flour
Research Overview
This project investigated the quality of pupuru, a fermented and smoke-dried cassava-based staple food, fortified with flour produced from coconut rhinoceros beetle (Oryctes rhinoceros) larvae.
The study evaluated how different levels of coconut beetle larvae flour affected the nutritional, physicochemical, functional, pasting, microbiological, and sensory properties of pupuru.
Research Objective
The study aimed to determine whether coconut beetle larvae flour could improve the nutritional quality of pupuru while maintaining acceptable sensory characteristics and microbiological safety.

Key Findings
Fortification substantially increased the protein content of pupuru. Crude protein increased from 2.94% in the control formulation to 16.34% at 25% substitution.
Mineral content also increased, including calcium, potassium, zinc, and iron. The amino acid profile showed improvement in several amino acids that are limited in cassava-based foods.
However, sensory acceptability decreased as the level of coconut beetle flour increased.
The 10% substitution level (PF90) provided the best overall balance between nutritional improvement and sensory acceptability.
